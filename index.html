Performance Stability System

Lead engineer, stability and controls. 2024.

A control system that supports operators in unstable conditions without taking the vehicle out from under them when it is safe to push. Heavy vehicles handling unbalanced or shifting loads sit in two danger zones: when the operator cannot feel a problem coming, and when an over conservative safety system removes performance the operator actually needs. The job was to find the line between them, preventing load spillage while keeping performance available.

Tools: Embedded C, MATLAB, CAN.

Smart Environment Sensor Plus (SEnS+)

Lead engineer, machine vision and braking. 2022.

Stereoscopic camera detection for pedestrians and obstacles, integrated directly with regenerative braking. The perception is not a notification, it is an action wired into the vehicle safety stack. Pedestrian aware perception is well understood in passenger cars, but less so in industrial machines, where false positives cost throughput and false negatives cost lives. SEnS+ had to tell a person from a stack of pallets in real time, then turn that detection into measurable braking force.

Tools: Stereo cameras, ML perception, regenerative brake demand.

Hydraulic Weight Sensing

Lead engineer, hydraulics and signal processing. 2023.

Real time load weighing using pressure transducers built into the hydraulic manifold. It drives operator display feedback and automatic speed reduction once the load crosses the rated envelope. Overloaded vehicles are a leading cause of preventable damage to product, machine, and operator, and conventional weighing needs a known stop or a separate scale. The aim was to make weighing happen continuously and invisibly, using the hydraulics already on the vehicle.

Tools: Inline pressure transducers, signal processing, operator display and speed governor.

Steer Differentiation System

Lead engineer, sensors and geometry. 2024.

Real time refinement of steering geometry using high fidelity sensor resolution, giving tighter handling envelopes without compromising the operator feel for the machine. Steering systems on industrial vehicles usually trade resolution for robustness: the sensors are coarse and the controllers compensate with smoothing. That works until you want to fuse steering data with stability or perception systems that need cleaner inputs. The goal here was to lift the resolution floor.

Tools: High resolution sensors, CAN bus.

Interface Logic and Access Control

Lead engineer, HMI and safety. 2023.

A re architecture of the vehicle menu system around safety classes and operator access levels. The interface itself decides which controls are reachable, given who is driving and what the vehicle is doing. Industrial vehicle interfaces accumulate options over years, until the menu becomes a hazard surface in itself. The redesign brief was to stop showing operators controls they should not touch, and stop burying the controls technicians need.

Structural Frame Redesign

Lead engineer, FEA and fatigue. 2022.

A tractor frame redesigned from the welds up for infinite fatigue life. It was FEA driven and validated with strain gauges in the field, so failure modes were designed out before they reached customers. Frame fatigue cracks had been appearing late in service life, after the warranty window but well inside the customer expectation of useful life. Each one was a service event and a brand event, so the fix had to remove the failure mode entirely rather than push it further down the road.

Tools: FEA, strain gauge validation.

3D Slicer and G-code Generator

Personal, Python and 3D printing. 2025 to 2026.

An STL to G-code pipeline written from first principles in Python, because off the shelf slicers do not let you slice the way I wanted to. The interesting question, what happens when the layer planes are not horizontal, lives below their abstractions, so I built a smaller pipeline that exposes every stage. You load an STL, set a layer height and resolution, then slice and generate G-code, and each stage emits and shows its own intermediate state. It is the foundation for a research effort on support reduction through tilted or curved slicing planes.

Tools: Python, PyQt, Matplotlib.

Additive Manufacturing and Investment Casting

RIT, AMPrint Center. Additive manufacturing, CFD, casting.

Pour molten aluminium into a 3D printed ceramic mold and get a metal water nozzle. In Fusion 360 I built the negative mold around the part, with pour holes at the top and drainage at the bottom, and rotated the whole mold by 2 degrees so a 90 degree overhang section stayed printable. To stop the mold warping when fired, I generated a truncated octahedron lattice shell in nTopology, so most struts run vertical with the print direction. Before casting, a transient CFD simulation with air and liquid aluminium at 1273 K checked whether the metal would fill the geometry without freezing or trapping air.

Tools: Fusion 360, nTopology, CFD.

The finished machine, 1200 by 800 mm work area with the water cooled spindle on the gantry.

Polymer Lithography Simulation

RIT Honors research, Dr. Padmanabhan. Molecular dynamics.

Block copolymer phase separation simulated to bridge the gap between simulation and experiment for microfabrication. Water like and oil like macromolecules joined into a block copolymer repel and align into patterns, and depending on the ratio and chemistry you get holes, spheres, stripes, or lamellae that are then used in lithography. Because each parameter, temperature, repulsion strength, and atom size, affects the structure chaotically, we ran thousands of simulations to find where to start experiments instead of running expensive lab runs blind. The model is about 100 lines of molecular dynamics using the Weeks Chandler Andersen potential, run in LAMMPS on the RIT research cluster, and the results showed that the interaction parameter falls with temperature and rises with repulsion strength.

Tools: LAMMPS, RIT research cluster, molecular dynamics, WCA potential.

Flight Dynamics Simulation

RIT coursework, Simulink and MATLAB.

Aircraft response simulated in Simulink and MATLAB, the same kind of analysis used on the experimental X-62 VISTA testbed. Each run simulates a trim condition or a stick, rudder, or aileron step at a fixed time step in ode45, logging true velocity, pitch, roll, and yaw rates, angle of attack, sideslip, and Euler angles. Nine aircraft were simulated across the same conditions, and the results can be compared directly against real flight test data to check whether the control system behaves as expected. I used a quaternion model over Euler angles because Euler angles suffer gimbal lock and integration singularities, which quaternions avoid at the cost of being less intuitive to read.

Tools: Simulink, MATLAB, ode45.

Double Pendulum Experiment

RIT coursework, dynamics and validation.

A theoretical model of a chaotic system, validated against a physical rig released from the same starting condition. The pendulum is two solid arms on bearings, with the top arm released from 45 degrees against a hard stopper, and the motion is captured at 120 frames per second. Colored dots are tracked in Kinovea, the arm angles are computed and fed into MATLAB, and a mean squared error and visual overlay compare measured against simulated, with a Monte Carlo envelope from 1000 runs. The model and rig agree for about a second and a half, and the disagreement after that quantifies how much you do not know about the bearing friction and the imperfect release. The target was under 10 percent error for the first two seconds.

Tools: MATLAB, Kinovea, Lagrangian model with ODE integration.

Vacuum Cannon Experiment

RIT coursework, fluid dynamics and validation.

Ping pong ball exit velocity predicted from compressible flow, then measured. Air is pumped out of a 1.52 m PVC pipe sealed at both ends with tape, the back is punctured, air rushes in, and the ball is shot out into a ballistic block that records displacement. The exit velocity is recovered with an Eulerian method that integrates the net pressure force over small time steps, and predicted values are compared against measured runs at 2 and 8 kPa across 10 runs. The model uses a Vena Contracta inlet coefficient and ignores friction and air bypassing the ball, and reading where it under or overshoots shows where the physics is missing, with friction the largest and cheapest discrepancy to fix.

Tools: MATLAB, Eulerian time step integration.

Saturn V Finite Element Analysis

Personal, FEA and structural. 2024.

A finite element study on the Saturn V, using Ansys to analyze the Rocketdyne F-1 engine nozzle from the Apollo 11 vehicle, which produced roughly 7.5 million pounds of thrust. The goal is to verify that the structure does not fail under its operating stresses, through static stress and modal analysis with worst case load envelopes. The writeup, including stress contours and mode shapes, is still pending.

Tools: Ansys, FEA.
