Performance Stability System

A control system that holds a heavy vehicle steady under an unbalanced or shifting load, without pulling performance back when it's safe to push. The whole job was finding the line between spilling a load and over-limiting an operator who needs the speed.

Performance Stability System

Lead engineer, stability and controls. 2024.

A control system that holds a heavy vehicle steady under a shifting load, without pulling performance back when it's safe to push.

Designed / Analyzed / Built / Proved
Designed

I mapped the two failure zones first: the operator who can't feel a tip coming, and the safety logic so conservative it removes speed the operator actually needs. The controller sits between them, reading load state off the CAN bus and deciding moment by moment how much performance to allow.

Analyzed

I ran PID tuning across the electric and hydraulic subsystems, lift and drive and steering, to cut lag and keep the response stable as the load changed. A static stability tool I wrote forecasts the outcome of the ANSI/ITSDF B56.1 tip tests, so we could evaluate a design before building it.

Built

The logic runs on the production vehicle controller and talks to the drivetrain over CAN.

Proved

Measured against the prior baseline, the system delivered 14% better performance and 10% less battery draw.

No field plot to post. The number is the deliverable, and it's on the resume.

Smart Environment Sensor Plus (SEnS+)

Lead engineer, machine vision and braking. 2022.

Stereoscopic camera detection for pedestrians, wired straight into regenerative braking. Not a beep on a screen, a braking action.

Designed / Analyzed / Built / Proved
Designed

I designed the sensing chain around a stereo camera pair, dynamic zoning that changes the alert region with vehicle speed and path, and control logic that turns a detection into a brake demand. The hard part was telling a person from a stack of pallets, because a false positive costs throughput and a false negative costs a life.

Analyzed

Pedestrian perception is solved in passenger cars and much rougher in a warehouse, so the analysis was about where the vision model fails and how to zone around it. I tuned the detection zones against the vehicle's stopping behavior so the brake assist triggers with room to stop.

Built

The sensor and its logic went into a shipping product.

Proved

Detection drives regenerative braking automatically, so an operator who misses a pedestrian still gets the stop.

Detection-rate and stopping-distance figures are internal. I can walk through them in an interview.

Hydraulic Weight Sensing

The vehicle weighs its own load. Pressure transducers built into the hydraulic manifold read the lift pressure, and signal processing turns that into a live weight on the operator display.

Hydraulic Weight Sensing

Lead engineer, hydraulics and signal processing. 2023.

Pressure transducers in the hydraulic manifold read lift pressure, and signal processing turns it into a live weight on the display.

Designed / Analyzed / Built / Proved
Designed

Instead of a separate scale or a known stop, I put the sensing where the force already is: transducers inline in the hydraulic manifold. Weighing then happens continuously off hardware the vehicle already carries.

Analyzed

I wrote the signal processing that converts manifold pressure into load weight, filtering out the noise from lift motion and hydraulic transients so the reading settles instead of jumping.

Built

No photo. The transducers sit inside the manifold on production hydraulics.

Proved

The weight feeds the operator display, and once a load crosses the rated envelope the system reduces speed on its own. Overloading is a top cause of preventable damage to product, machine, and operator, and this catches it before the lift.

Steer Differentiation System

Lead engineer, sensors and geometry. 2024.

AC steering logic that lets the drive tires steer at different rates, tightening the turn without making the machine feel twitchy.

Designed / Analyzed / Built / Proved
Designed

I developed the AC steering control logic for drive-tire steer differentiation, using higher sensor resolution to refine the steering geometry in real time. The design target was a tighter handling envelope that still preserved the operator's feel for the machine.

Analyzed

Industrial steering usually trades resolution for robustness, so the sensors are coarse and the controller smooths over them. That falls apart once you want to fuse steering data into stability or perception systems that need clean inputs, so I worked on lifting the resolution floor.

Built

Proved

Differentiating the steer angle across the drive tires reduced the turning radius and reduced tire wear.

Additive Manufacturing and Investment Casting

Pour molten aluminium into a 3D printed ceramic mold and get a metal water nozzle back out. The trick is keeping the printed mold both printable and strong enough not to warp when it's fired.

Additive Manufacturing and Investment Casting

RIT, AMPrint Center. Fusion 360, nTopology, CFD.

Cast a metal water nozzle from a 3D printed ceramic mold, kept printable with a 2 degree tilt and strong with a lattice shell.

Designed / Analyzed / Built / Proved
Designed

In Fusion 360 I built the negative mold around the part, pour holes at the top and drainage at the bottom, then rotated the whole mold 2 degrees so a 90 degree overhang section stayed printable. To stop the shell warping in the kiln, I generated a truncated-octahedron lattice in nTopology so most struts run vertical with the print direction.

Analyzed

Before committing metal, I ran a transient CFD simulation with air and liquid aluminium at 1273 K to check whether the pour would fill the geometry without freezing early or trapping air.

Built

The mold prints and the pour is designed. I don't have a clean photo of the finished cast nozzle yet.

Proved

The CFD showed a complete fill before any aluminium was melted, which is the whole point of simulating a pour: you find the cold shut or the trapped-air pocket on the screen, not in a ruined mold.

Polymer Lithography Simulation

Block copolymer phase separation, simulated to bridge the gap between simulation and experiment for microfabrication. Join a water-like and an oil-like macromolecule into one chain and they repel and self organize into patterns you can print with.

Polymer Lithography Simulation

RIT Honors research, Dr. Padmanabhan, 2020. LAMMPS.

Block copolymer phase separation in LAMMPS, mapping which patterns form so the lab knows where to start.

Designed / Analyzed / Built / Proved
Designed

Depending on the block ratio and chemistry, the same system settles into holes, spheres, stripes, or lamellae, and those shapes are what lithography then uses. The design space is which pattern you get.

Analyzed

The model is about 100 lines of molecular dynamics using the Weeks-Chandler-Andersen potential, run in LAMMPS on the RIT research cluster. Temperature, repulsion strength, and atom size each push the outcome chaotically, so we ran thousands of simulations to find where to start experiments instead of running expensive lab runs blind.

Built

The build here is the simulation itself, the WCA model and its LAMMPS runs. No physical rig.

Proved

The runs reached clean separated end states, and the results showed the interaction parameter falls with temperature and rises with repulsion strength. That's a starting map for the lab instead of a blind search.

Flight Dynamics Simulation

Flight Dynamics Simulation

RIT coursework. Simulink, MATLAB, ode45.

Aircraft response in Simulink, built on a quaternion model, run across nine aircraft and checked against flight-test data.

Designed / Analyzed / Built / Proved
Designed

I built a quaternion attitude model rather than Euler angles, because Euler angles hit gimbal lock and integration singularities. Quaternions avoid that, at the cost of being harder to read off the page.

Analyzed

A run applies a stick, rudder, or aileron step at a fixed time step and logs true velocity, pitch, roll and yaw rates, angle of attack, sideslip, and Euler angles. I ran nine different aircraft through the same conditions.

Built

This one is a model, not hardware. The build is the Simulink and MATLAB code that runs the nine configurations.

Proved

The logged response curves can be compared directly against real flight-test data to check the control behavior.

Double Pendulum Experiment

A theoretical model of a chaotic system, checked against a physical rig released from the same starting point. The target was under 10 percent error for the first two seconds.

Double Pendulum Experiment

RIT coursework, dynamics and validation. MATLAB, Kinovea.

A Lagrangian model of a chaotic pendulum, tracked at 120 fps and checked against the rig with a Monte Carlo band.

Designed / Analyzed / Built / Proved
Designed

I derived a Lagrangian model of the two-arm pendulum and integrated it as a set of ODEs in MATLAB. Chaos means the interesting question isn't whether it matches, it's how long before it stops matching.

Analyzed

I captured the rig at 120 frames per second, tracked colored dots on each arm in Kinovea, and computed the arm angles to feed back into MATLAB. A Monte Carlo envelope from 1000 runs sets the band on the prediction.

Built

Two solid arms on bearings, with the top arm released from 45 degrees against a hard stopper for a repeatable start.

Proved

Model and rig agree for about a second and a half. What happens after that is the useful part: the disagreement quantifies how much I don't know about the bearing friction and the imperfect release.

Vacuum Cannon Experiment

Predict a ping pong ball's exit velocity from compressible flow, then go measure it. Pump the air out of a sealed tube, punch the back open, and the pressure difference fires the ball.

Vacuum Cannon Experiment

RIT coursework, fluid dynamics and validation. MATLAB.

Predict a ping pong ball's exit velocity from compressible flow, then measure it across 10 runs at 2 and 8 kPa.

Designed / Analyzed / Built / Proved
Designed

The setup is a 1.52 m PVC pipe sealed at both ends with tape, a vacuum pump, and a ballistic block that records displacement when the ball lands.

Analyzed

I recovered the exit velocity with an Eulerian method that integrates the net pressure force over small time steps, using a Vena Contracta inlet coefficient. The model ignores friction and air slipping past the ball, which is deliberate: those gaps are where the error will show up.

Built

The physical cannon is the taped PVC tube and pump in the setup above. No separate build photo.

Proved

I compared predicted against measured across 10 runs at 2 and 8 kPa. Reading where it over or undershoots points straight at the missing physics, and friction is the largest and cheapest of those to add next.
