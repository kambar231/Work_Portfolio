ON BUILDING
A TOOLBOX

Stephen King has an idea I keep coming back to: his grandfather hauled a heavy, multi-tier toolbox to every job so he was never running back for the one thing he forgot — he showed up already carrying everything. You spend a life stocking a box like that, most-used tools on top, and carry it so the work never catches you empty-handed.

I'm an engineer, not a novelist — but the box is the same. Everything below is what I've put in mine. The top tray is what I open every day; the deeper you go, the older and slower-earned the tools get.

The hand that fills the box

I learned the language and the machine shop at roughly the same speed, picked up a BS and a master's in Mechanical Engineering from RIT — controls concentration, because that's the part that wouldn't leave me alone — and now I design mechanical and control systems for industrial vehicles at The Raymond Corporation. None of this makes me a finished engineer; King's right that owning good tools isn't the same as being a great builder. It just means that when the next interesting problem shows up, I'm not standing there empty-handed. So I keep stocking the box — curiosity is the one tool I'll never finish using.

If something in the box made you want to ask a question, build a thing, or just argue about quaternions — the drawer's open.
