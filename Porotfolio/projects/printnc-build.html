PrintNC Build (CNC)

An industrial-grade router you can build in a garage — or on the third floor of an apartment complex.

I wanted a machine that could handle more than just wood, so I went with the PrintNC — a hybrid beast that uses a heavy steel frame for rigidity and 3D-printed parts for precision alignment. It's essentially an industrial-grade router you can build in a garage or on the third floor of an apartment complex. Do not tell my landlord :)

Spec sheet, in plain English

Work area: 1200 × 800 mm — big enough to do real work. Spindle: 2.2 kW high-frequency, water-cooled — quiet, doesn't burn up under load. Tolerance: ±0.1 mm — accurate enough to cut a fitted bearing seat, not accurate enough to be a Swiss watch.

Phase I — Design & Purchase

In Fusion 360 I customised the machine's footprint for my shop space. BOM was defined and parts were ordered. I always define a step-by-step guide for how everything will be built — this project was an exception, because the PrintNC community already has great documentation.

Phase II — Fabrication & Assembly

This was the core physical build. I cut the steel members to length, then drilled and tapped the mounting points for the motion hardware. To keep everything square during assembly, I used high-infill 3D-printed components that acted as both structural joins and motor mounts.

Phase III — Electronics & Controls

Integrated a 2.2 kW spindle and VFD, which required a dedicated EMI line filter to stop electrical noise from feeding back into the logic circuits. Used shielded 4-core cabling for the motors and grounded them at a single star point to avoid ground loops. After flashing the controller firmware, I calibrated the motor drivers and mapped the I/O for limit switches and emergency stops, so the machine was both precise and safe for long operations.
